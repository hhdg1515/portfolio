Asset Library UX

An early exploration of browsing, retention and storage for uploaded media.

Role
Product designer, via design agency
Duration
Late 2023 – April 2024
Team
Agency design team + OpusClip design counterpart
Status
Concept presented; adoption unknown
Proof Context Decisions Takeaways

Original Figma interface states.

All supported broad scanning, Videos prioritized duration, and Projects prioritized file count.

One route for finding, retaining and reusing uploaded media.

At the time, users submitted a long-video URL, spent credits and received AI-selected short clips. The Asset Library did not yet exist, and my assignment was not a finished PRD. I made one end-to-end route concrete enough for the team to evaluate its browsing model, storage lifecycle and boundary with generated work.

Competitive review
I personally compared Runway, Canva, Vimeo and VEED before developing the Figma route.
Design scope
Browsing states, media classification, expiry, plan capacity and storage warnings.
Scope boundary
Later AI B-roll, final pricing, engineering architecture, launch ownership and post-launch metrics were outside this work.
Design decisions

One library needed more than one way in.

These early frames assumed the Library would hold video material. A dropdown filtered All, Footage or B-roll, while grid and list views tested visual browsing against denser source, date, duration and expiry metadata. The later route shown above moved broader categories into persistent tabs.

Storage raised an unresolved business-model question.

I visualized one possible route for communicating expiry, plan capacity and additional storage. The original Figma states show a media expiry, a low-use plan, an orange warning after more than half was used and a red near-limit state. Credits remain unchanged in the plan examples.

All brought different models together without mixing them.

I proposed All as a grouped overview: Folders first, Projects next, followed by individual Videos and Images. Projects reused the existing clipping-history model, keeping each source video with its generated clips, while reusable files remained in their own sections. This was an information-architecture proposal; I had not evaluated backend storage cost or implementation.

Evidence boundary Presented through the agency engagement. No participant-based usability study, adoption data or later product outcome is claimed.

Takeaways
  • One label can hide distinct models: reusable inputs, project history and generated outputs.
  • Storage screens make expiry, healthy capacity and approaching limits visible.
